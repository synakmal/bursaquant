How does the accumulation checklist work?

A quiet accumulation candidate requires a narrow 20-day base, contracting movement, positive CMF20, positive directional-volume pressure, liquidity and a price still below and near the preceding 20-day high. A breakout candidate instead requires a close beyond that prior high with relative-volume confirmation.

CMF20 weights where each candle closes inside its high–low range by volume. Directional-volume pressure compares volume on rising and falling closes. Both are estimates of pressure: every transaction has a buyer and a seller, and these readings cannot identify institutions or establish their intent.

Contraction compares the latest 20-day range or 14-day average true-range percentage with the preceding window. The simple true-range average here differs from Wilder's ATR shown on the analysis page. Distribution pressure requires both negative close-location and directional-volume readings.

At least 60 completed daily candles and usable volume are required. The checklist score is a count of weighted conditions, not a probability of profit. Missing data, corporate actions, poor liquidity and false breakouts can mislead; the rules have not been backtested. Daily candles older than seven calendar days are excluded as stale. Completion is inferred by the gateway.

Use the findings to build a research shortlist. Review the underlying chart, current bid/ask, announcements and your risk plan before making a decision. Known warrant codes and identified derivatives are excluded pending a separate model. Verify security types in custom ticker lists, especially US symbols whose ticker alone does not identify a warrant.
